If you are flying to, from or within Japan, the rules for power banks changed on April 24, 2026. The number you can carry is now limited to two, and using a power bank onboard to charge anything is prohibited. This guide summarizes the rules as announced by Japan's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT) and explained by Japan Airlines (JAL) and Japanese media.

*This article summarizes public information as of November 2026. Rules can change and airlines may apply stricter rules, so confirm with your airline and the official MLIT announcement before you fly.*

## The key rules

**Before boarding, do not:**

- put a power bank in checked baggage
- bring a power bank rated above 160Wh into the cabin
- carry a power bank without protection against short circuits

**After boarding, do not:**

- store a power bank in the overhead compartment
- charge a power bank from the aircraft's power outlet
- use a power bank to charge another device (phone, tablet and so on)

**How many:** up to **two power banks per person**.

Breaking these rules can result in penalties under Japan's Civil Aeronautics Act, according to MLIT. Some airlines apply stricter limits of their own.

## Power banks vs. spare lithium-ion batteries

The rules separate "power banks" (batteries built to charge other devices) from "spare lithium-ion batteries" (for example, a spare battery taken out of a camera). How many spare batteries you can bring depends on the power banks you carry:

- **Two power banks of 100Wh or less:** spare batteries up to 100Wh have no limit; spare batteries over 100Wh up to 160Wh, maximum two
- **Two power banks over 100Wh up to 160Wh:** spare batteries up to 100Wh have no limit; spare batteries over 100Wh up to 160Wh are not allowed
- **One power bank of 100Wh or less and one over 100Wh up to 160Wh:** spare batteries up to 100Wh have no limit; spare batteries over 100Wh up to 160Wh, maximum one

If you travel with camera batteries, check this combination carefully.

## How to convert mAh to Wh

Most power banks list capacity in mAh, but the rules use Wh. MLIT gives this formula:

**Wh = rated capacity (mAh) x rated voltage (V) / 1000**

Many lithium-ion cells are rated around 3.6 to 3.7V. Using 3.7V as an example:

- 10,000mAh = 37Wh
- 20,000mAh = 74Wh
- 26,800mAh = about 99.2Wh
- 30,000mAh = 111Wh (falls in the "over 100Wh, up to 160Wh" group)

Voltage differs by product, so use the Wh figure printed on the unit or in the manual when you can.

## Choosing a power bank for travel

These are rules of thumb, not official advice:

- A power bank of **100Wh or less** leaves you more room for spare batteries.
- A unit with a **Wh rating printed on it** is easier to check at the airport.
- If you carry several devices, one larger power bank can replace two smaller ones and keeps you within the two-unit limit.

## Pre-flight checklist

- Wh rating (or mAh and voltage) is visible on the unit
- 160Wh or less per unit
- Two units or fewer
- Nothing in checked baggage
- Terminals protected (case, pouch or tape)
- Phone and power bank charged before boarding
- Your airline's own rules checked

## Foreign airlines

Rules differ by country and airline. According to Japanese aviation media, some airlines announced in 2025 that they prohibit using or charging power banks onboard. Always check the rules of every airline on your itinerary.
