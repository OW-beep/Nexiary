Mouse skates, also called mouse feet, are the small pads on the underside of a mouse. They wear down with use, and many players replace them to restore glide or change the feel of the mouse. With so many materials, thicknesses and shapes available, it helps to know what to check before you buy. This guide organizes the main points from manufacturers' announcements and gaming guides.

*This is a buying guide based on public information, not a hands-on test of specific products.*

## 1. Check compatibility first

Skates are shaped and positioned for specific mice, so many are sold as model-specific sets. For example, GRAPHT's PTFE and glass mouse skates were released for specific models such as the Razer DeathAdder V3 HyperSpeed. Look for your mouse model on the product's compatibility list. If your model is not listed, you may need cut-to-fit skates.

## 2. Material: PTFE vs. glass

- **PTFE (fluoropolymer):** low friction, used on many gaming mice, and often recommended as the first material to try because it balances glide and stopping control. PTFE wears down with use, so skates are consumables.
- **Glass:** very low friction and a light glide. Some guides mention that it can feel harder to stop, and it is said to wear less.

Other materials, such as polyethylene or metal, also exist.

## 3. Thickness

Thickness affects the height of the mouse and the sensor's lift-off distance (how high you can raise the mouse before the cursor stops tracking). Some guides suggest around 0.7mm is easy to handle, and GRAPHT's PTFE skates are 0.8mm. If the thickness differs a lot from your stock skates, the feel can change, so stay close to the original unless you want a different feel.

## 4. Match with your mousepad

The feel also depends on the mousepad. It is commonly said that PTFE and glass skates are easy to pair with cloth pads, and that glass skates on a hard pad can feel too slippery. Dirt and dust on the pad can also affect glide and wear, so cleaning the pad helps.

## Checklist

- Is my mouse model on the compatibility list?
- What material is it (PTFE, glass, other)?
- Is the thickness close to my stock skates?
- Does it suit my mousepad (cloth or hard)?
- What is in the set (one mouse worth, or spares)?

## If you are not sure

For a first replacement, PTFE skates with a thickness close to the original are the safe pick. If you later want lighter glide or longer life, you can try other materials.
